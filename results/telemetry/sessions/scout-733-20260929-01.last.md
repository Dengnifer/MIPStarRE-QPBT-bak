### Derivation of the selected mathematical mechanism

Let \(C(A,B)\) denote consistency defect and \(D(A,B)\) squared state-dependent distance. Expectations and outcome sums use the original distributions and normalization.

For complete measurements,
\[
2C(A,B)=D(A,B)+u_A+u_B,\qquad u_A,u_B\ge0.
\]
This supplies two useful consequences:
\[
C(A,D)\le3\bigl(C(A,B)+C(C,B)+C(C,D)\bigr),
\tag{1}
\]
and, if \(P\) is projective,
\[
D(P,A)\le R,\quad C(A,B)\le\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C(P,B)\le R+2\delta.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed,
\[
2C(P,B)
 \le2D(P,A)+2D(A,B)+u_B
 \le2R+4\delta.
\]

These are statements about complete measurements. They must not be exported as unrestricted submeasurement estimates.

**Projective pasting.** Use the paper’s distribution on \((x,y_1,y_2)\), conditional collision error \(\eta\), joint projective predictor \(A\), and two forward marginal consistency bounds \(\delta\). For this refinement, assume both \(G_1,G_2\) are projective.

Suppressing tensor placements and question indices, write:

- \(P_a\) for the evaluated first codeword measurement;
- \(G_g\) for the fine second codeword measurement;
- \(Q_b=\sum_{g:g(y_2)=b}G_g\);
- \(H_g\) for the **constructed Schmidt mirror** of \(G_g\) on the opposite register.

The mirror is projective and commutes with every Bob operator. It depends on \(x\), not on \(y_2\). The family \(P\) depends only on \((x,y_1)\). These dependencies are precisely what the conditional collision argument requires.

The existing mirror and collision lemmas give
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_c&:=\mathbb E\sum_b\|(Q_b-H_b)\psi\|^2\le4\delta,\\
E_f&:=\mathbb E\sum_g\|(G_g-H_g)\psi\|^2\le4\delta+2\eta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The existing coarse commutator bound gives
\[
K_0:=\mathbb E\sum_{a,b}\|[P_a,Q_b]\psi\|^2\le32\delta.
\tag{4}
\]

Define the correlations after measuring \(P\):
\[
M_c=\mathbb E\sum_{a,b}\langle H_bP_aQ_bP_a\rangle,\qquad
M_f=\mathbb E\sum_{a,g}\langle H_gP_aG_gP_a\rangle.
\]
Projectivity and completeness give the exact identities
\[
\mathbb E\sum_{a,b}\|(Q_b-H_b)P_a\psi\|^2=2(1-M_c),
\]
\[
\mathbb E\sum_{a,g}\|(G_g-H_g)P_a\psi\|^2=2(1-M_f).
\tag{5}
\]

Now
\[
(Q_b-H_b)P_a=[Q_b,P_a]+P_a(Q_b-H_b).
\]
Applying the squared-norm triangle and summing over the projective family \(P\) yields
\[
1-M_c\le K_0+E_c.
\tag{6}
\]
The existing pinched collision identity and bound give
\[
M_c-M_f\le\eta,
\qquad
1-M_f\le K_0+E_c+\eta.
\tag{7}
\]

For the fine commutator,
\[
[P_a,G_g]=P_a(G_g-H_g)+(H_g-G_g)P_a.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
K_f
&:=\mathbb E\sum_{a,g}\|[P_a,G_g]\psi\|^2\\
&\le2E_f+4(1-M_f)\\
&\le2E_f+4K_0+4E_c+4\eta\\
&\le152\delta+8\eta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

This replaces the current comparison of scalar cross terms, which takes square roots.

Finally, let \(A_a\) be the first marginal of the joint predictor. Its defect against the pinched measurement is exactly
\[
C\!\left(A,\sum_gG_gP_aG_g\right)
 =\mathbb E\sum_{a,g}\|(I-A_a)P_aG_g\psi\|^2.
\]
Insert \(P_aG_g=G_gP_a+[P_a,G_g]\). Projectivity, completeness and opposite-register commutation give
\[
C\!\left(A,\sum_gG_gP_aG_g\right)
 \le2C(A,P)+2K_f
 \le2\delta+2K_f.
\tag{9}
\]
The existing pasted-to-pinched reduction adds the second marginal defect:
\[
\boxed{C(A_{\rm joint},J)\le3\delta+2K_f
      \le307\delta+16\eta.}
\tag{10}
\]

No register-exchange assumption was used. The mirror is constructed internally. The heterogeneous version follows through the existing common-space padding identities, which preserve the defects and sandwich effects exactly.

The three substantive pasting improvements are now explicit:

1. retain the proved linear mirror estimate;
2. compare the post-measurement vectors in squared norm before evaluating overlaps;
3. estimate pinching failure as a sum of squared failure amplitudes.

They concern the same sandwich measurement. This is stronger than merely retaining the mirror estimate and leaving the remaining Cauchy–Schwarz roots in place.
